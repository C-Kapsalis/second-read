# Blog posts and articles: hard checks

Review what decides whether the piece gets found, opened and read: the title
and meta description, the intro, and the structure. Then the claims. Count
characters and words. If only an excerpt is pasted, judge what you see and
say so.

## Checks

| # | Check | Fails when | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Headline promise | The title promises what the body does not deliver ("7 ways" with 5 sections; "the complete guide" with two sections) | Blocker |
| B2 | Title tag | Over 60 characters (cut in search results); missing the target keyword; keyword repeated 3 or more times | Major (Minor for length alone) |
| B3 | Meta description | Missing; over 160 characters; does not say what the reader gets | Minor (Major when missing) |
| B4 | Intro gets to the point | The first 2-3 sentences (about 60 words) do not say what the reader will learn or the answer; throat-clearing opener ("In today's fast-paced digital landscape", a definition nobody asked for, the company's history) | Major |
| B5 | Keyword placement | The target keyword is missing from the title, the H1, the first 100 words, or every H2; the URL slug lacks it | Major for title, H1 or intro; Minor for H2 or slug |
| B6 | Heading structure | No H1 or more than one (count `#` marks: a title written as `## ` is an H2, not an H1); a skipped level (H2 straight to H4); no H2 in over 300 words | Major |
| B7 | Scannable | A paragraph over 100 words; a sequence of steps or options written as prose; average sentence over 25 words | Minor (Major when the main point is buried) |
| B8 | Numbers are right | A percentage, total or change that does not compute ("2.0% to 3.0%, a 33% increase"); the same figure given two ways (sample of 1,200 here, 800 there) | Blocker |
| B9 | Sources | A statistic or study with no source, or a dated one presented as current (a figure from more than about 3 years ago with no year) | Major |
| B10 | Claims | Health, money, legal or safety claims ("cures", "guaranteed"); a claim about a named competitor with no source | Blocker |
| B11 | Disclosure | Affiliate links or sponsorship with no disclosure; a comparison written by one of the compared companies that calls itself unbiased and does not say who wrote it | Blocker for undisclosed affiliate links; Major otherwise |
| B12 | Stage fit | The CTA does not fit the stage: an awareness article ending in "Buy now" or a fake deadline; a comparison or decision article with no next step | Major |
| B13 | Finished | "TK", "[link to ...]", "[INSERT ...]", comments to the editor, an empty section | Blocker |
| B14 | Stuffing | The keyword more than about 3 times in a 50-word stretch, or over 10% of words | Major |
| B15 | Plain language | Acronyms or jargon the stated audience may not know, left undefined; template phrases ("delve", "navigate the landscape", "It's important to note", "In conclusion") | Minor (Major for a non-expert audience) |
| B16 | Ending | No conclusion or next step; ends mid-argument | Minor |
| B17 | Images and links | Images with no alt text; internal links with "click here" as anchor text | Minor |

Search intent decides the format: "what is", "how to" and "tips" queries
(awareness) want answers and steps; "best X for Y", "A vs B" and "reviews"
(consideration) want honest comparison; "deal", "discount", "buy" queries
(action) belong on product and landing pages, not blog posts. One primary
keyword per article; if the user says another post targets the same keyword,
flag cannibalisation (Major). Substantial, original, current content ranks;
thin or copied sections and stale facts do not.

## Rewrite rules for blogs

Give: title tag (with count), meta description (with count), H1, a new
intro (the point in the first two sentences), and the H2 outline with one
line per section. Rewrite paragraphs in full only where a fix needs it.
Keep every statistic only with its ledger source; add [source] where it is
missing. Never invent a study, a quote, an expert or a year.

## Usually fine in a blog (do not flag)

A numbered list for steps; a first-person anecdote opening that reaches the
point within three sentences; a soft CTA (newsletter, related post) on an
awareness article; a "talk to your doctor" line; a sourced statistic with its
year; a stated limitation; internal links to related posts with descriptive
anchors; a comparison that admits where the competitor wins; a long article
whose length fits its intent.

## You cannot check from pasted copy

Rankings, search volume, competition, load speed, mobile layout, whether
links work, whether the content is copied elsewhere. Name them under "Not
checked".
