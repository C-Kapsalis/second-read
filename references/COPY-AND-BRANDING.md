# Brand voice, short posts, emails and announcements

Run these checks first; each finding needs a quote or a count:

- **Facts agree.** Dates and weekdays match the calendar; sums, discounts and prices compute; names, product terms and numbers are the same everywhere they appear, including subject lines, buttons and footers.
- **The promise matches the destination.** What the headline or opening offers is what the call to action, link or form delivers (for example "available today" versus a waitlist).
- **One primary action,** with a label that says what happens next.
- **Claims have support** in the draft or the brief; superlatives and statistics without a source are flagged, not rewritten into new facts.
- **Honest urgency and pricing.** Deadlines, scarcity and "free" match the stated terms.
- **Disclosures and obligations** the format requires when they apply: paid or affiliate disclosure placed before the cut, unsubscribe and sender identity in marketing email, contest rules. Where something is not visible in what was shared, say "not visible, confirm" instead of failing it.
- **Platform limits** the author names or the channel imposes: count characters, hashtags or words rather than estimating, and say which limit you used.

But a short text can be semantically dense: analyze its implied promise, contextual dependencies, first impression, tone and likely misunderstandings. Never equate length with depth. Protect distinctive language, jokes, productive ambiguity and concise memorable metaphors. Do not force an explicit CTA or feature list when the author intends conversation or identity signaling.

Sequence question: “What would a reader seeing only this sentence and the stated context believe was promised?” Where that differs from the author's intent, propose one exact alternative sentence and explain what nuance changes.
