# Editorial restraint: a good editor rejects bad edits

Every proposed change competes against **leave unchanged**. A polished sentence is not automatically an improvement; an intentional rough edge can be effective. The burden is on the proposed edit to show a real reader benefit without reducing distinctiveness or accuracy.

## Two-pass self-review

1. Draft the smallest edit that addresses a checkable problem or meaningful reader consequence.
2. Argue against your own edit: Does it add a disclaimer already implicit in “imagine” or “consider”? Replace an image with jargon? Over-explain subtext? Create repetition? Dilute a memorable phrase? Introduce unsupported data, a stronger promise or premature resolution? Enforce an irrelevant house rule?
3. If the second pass wins, mark **leave** and suppress the finding. If both are plausible, label optional and give a minimal alternative only.

Do not label prose “AI-generated” or infer the author's identity from style. Reference `NATURAL-VOICE.md` only when demonstrable language habits reduce clarity, specificity or reader trust.

## Contrastive examples

**Leave — hypothetical illustration already signals itself**
- Draft: “Imagine a nurse who finishes work at midnight and waits for a bus.”
- Reject: “This is a hypothetical scenario and does not reflect statistically representative data.”
- Reason: the opening word signals imagination; disclaimer costs pacing and fixes no confusion. Only intervene if later prose calls it proof of a citywide frequency.

**Leave — metaphor earns its place**
- Draft: “A timetable is a promise.”
- Reject: “A public transport schedule establishes a customer expectation.”
- Reason: the image clearly expresses a reader-relevant claim without an invented statistic.

**Edit — empty opening**
- Draft: “In today's fast-paced digital world, businesses must adapt to the ever-changing landscape.”
- Proposed if brief concerns delayed onboarding: “Customers are leaving setup halfway through. Here's what the next release changes.”
- Reason: replaces ungrounded sweeping rhetoric with supplied concrete relevance. Do not use this proposed replacement unless the brief documents those facts.

**Leave — literary ambiguity**
- Draft: “She put the key down. Neither of them reached for it.”
- Reject: “They were both afraid to confront their strained relationship.”
- Reason: interpretation isn't an authorized correction; ambiguity creates the scene's effect.

**Edit — incorrect terminology**
- Docs consistently call a feature “Snapshots,” but one command step says “Backups” while referring to the same UI item. Correct to the verified product label; don't rewrite the rest of the passage.

**Leave — a concise, specific CTA**
- “View pricing” may already accurately name the linked page. Don't demand “Unlock your success today” to make it more persuasive.

## Output restraint

When there are no material issues, report ready with no invented proposals and no forced quiz. For a strong draft with one useful optional adjustment, show only that. Do not auto-apply stylistic suggestions to the author's approved draft.
