# Natural voice, specificity and anti-formulaic prose

Use for **every content pathway**, but surface only actual defects. This is editing for **linguistic quality**, not AI-authorship detection. No stylistic fingerprint reliably proves how a passage was written; do not label text as AI-generated, assign a probability, or penalize polished grammar or multilingual writers.

## Signals worth inspecting, not automatically deleting

| Feature | Why it sometimes harms a piece | Legitimate use |
|---|---|---|
| “In today's fast-paced world,” “now more than ever” | Delays the actual claim; says little | Deliberate satirical or genre opening |
| “Unlock,” “revolutionize,” “seamless,” “game-changing” | Claims value without observable benefit | Actual product naming or documented quote |
| Repeated “Furthermore,” “Moreover,” “In conclusion” | Mechanical signposting obscures real logical relations | Long technical or instructional documents |
| “It's not just X; it's Y” repeated throughout | A formula stands in for explanation | A single crisp rhetorical contrast |
| Three parallel adjectives or clauses in every paragraph | Artificial rhythm, little new information | Deliberate speech cadence or brand voice |
| “Delve into,” “landscape,” “tapestry,” “pivotal” | Inflates abstract language | Precise literal usage or purposeful metaphor |
| Decorative em dashes and colon-heavy fragments | Breaks rather than organizes thought | Effective characteristic author cadence |
| Generic reassurance and over-hedging | Blurs recommendation or hides uncertainty | Real uncertainty that must remain |
| Restating the thesis after every section | Makes prose feel padded | Signposting complex instruction or controlled refrain |

## Operation rule

Every flag must provide (a) an exact quote, (b) the reader cost, (c) a shorter/more specific version consistent with the source and house voice, and (d) a check that the replacement **does not fabricate precision**. Prefer deleting a sentence that adds nothing to inventing a shiny concrete detail.

## Examples

**Edit — empty claim**
- Original: “Our cutting-edge solution empowers teams to leverage innovation.”
- If the brief states the product checks documentation commands, revise: “Check whether the setup commands in your documentation actually run.”
- Do not claim measurable speed or reliability without evidence.

**Leave — memorable parallelism**
- “Read it twice. Send it once.”
- This is balanced, short, and memorable. Formula-like structure alone is not a reason to change it.

**Leave — clear standard English**
- “The results were inconclusive.”
- Do not change to slang or introduce deliberate errors in an attempt to sound “human.”

**Edit — noncommittal padding**
- Original: “It is important to note that some teams may potentially encounter certain challenges.”
- If supported by the brief: “Some teams cannot complete setup without an API key.” Otherwise ask what the challenges actually are; don't invent them.

**Leave — recurring device in a speech**
- A carefully placed refrain can deepen an argument. Remove only repetition that loses its force or obscures new claims.

When editing a writer using English as an additional language, preserve personal diction, register, and legitimate regional variation. Explain a necessary idiom correction without treating accent or language background as a flaw.
