# Semantic precision: defend the author's meaning

Use this reference on substantive edits, especially conclusions, product promises, policy proposals and data-backed claims. For every proposed replacement, compare **what was asserted before and what would be asserted after**. An edit is not acceptable merely because it reads more smoothly.

## High-risk changes

| Dimension | Dangerous shift | Editing action |
|---|---|---|
| Quantifier/scope | “some” → “all”; one region → everywhere | Restore bounded population; avoid new prevalence |
| Modality | “could” → “will”; “consider” → “commit” | Preserve epistemic and normative force |
| Time | “next month” → “immediately”; trial → permanent rollout | Match brief, timeline, conditionality |
| Cause | correlates with → causes; anecdote → general proof | Rephrase as observation or add sourcing request |
| Presupposition | “when we launch” assumes approval | Prefer “if approved” when approval is unknown |
| Agent | “users can export” → “we export for you” | Preserve who performs the action |
| Metric | lifetime → annual; gross → net; nominal → real | Require metric and denominator to travel with figure |
| Comparison | faster than X → fastest in category | Retain comparison class and evidence |
| Obligation | “may” → “must” | Distinguish advice, requirement, law and preference |
| Referent | “it” acquires a new antecedent after a move | Clarify minimal reference without distorting voice |

Maintain separate labels for fact, cited evidence, interpretation, value judgment, prediction, metaphor and hypothetical illustration. No metaphor should automatically become a testable empirical proposition.

## Before/after decision pairs

**Edit — unsupported promise**
- Source: “The product should support offline export next quarter.”
- Bad proposed rewrite: “Offline export launches next quarter.”
- Safe revision: “The team proposes offline export for next quarter.”
- Why: proposal != approved roadmap or shipped feature.

**Leave alone — deliberate rhetorical force**
- Source: “A timetable is a promise. When the bus doesn't come, the city breaks it.”
- Bad revision: “Service arrival variability may negatively affect perceptions of municipal performance.”
- Preserve: the metaphor is clear, non-statistical and gives stakes.

**Edit — data scope**
- Source: “In our pilot, 8 of 10 participants completed setup.” Followed by “Most customers can set up instantly.”
- Change only the unsupported generalization, e.g. “In the small pilot, eight participants completed setup”; ask for timing evidence if “instantly” matters. Do not infer customer-wide outcomes from a pilot.

**Leave alone — warranted uncertainty**
- Source: “The early results suggest fewer errors, but the sample is small.”
- Do not strengthen to “The new design reduces errors.” The caution is doing important work.

**Edit — policy commitment**
- Source: “We need to test whether reliability or price matters more.” Final: “That proves fares must be removed.”
- Rewrite the conclusion **only after grounding the author's actual policy intention**. Do not decide which policy is desirable on the author's behalf.

## Semantic diff before publishing

Check each accepted operation for: changed actor, object, beneficiary, target group, domain, time, quantity, certainty, evidence level, condition, promise, and implied next action. If any changed, require author intent/supplied source that permits the change. A grammatically correct sentence that alters one of these fields is a regression, not an improvement.

If a substantive choice is unresolved, produce conditional edit alternatives or one targeted question. In ordinary chat the assembled draft containing required edits is a **provisional preview**, never an assertion that the author accepted a change.
