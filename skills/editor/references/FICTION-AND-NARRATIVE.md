# Fiction and personal narrative

Track chronology, point of view, scene goal, reversals, causal consequences, emotional stakes and thematic echoes. Highlight related scenes without explaining away deliberate mystery or figurative meaning. Suggest a moved beat, a clarified reference, or a cut only when it makes a desired effect more legible. Do not demand citations, explicit argument labels or a “one message” thesis from literary material. Avoid replacing the author's diction and cadence with standardized smoothness.

Sequence question: “What is a first-time reader entitled to infer about the character at this moment, before the reveal?” When divergence undermines the scene, offer an exact change; when divergence is suspense, leave it alone.
