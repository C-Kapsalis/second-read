# Policy, contested issues and public argument

Map factual observations, forecasts, normative commitments, proposed policies, conditions and uncertainty without converting one into another. Represent strong counterarguments fairly and avoid unsupported claims about actors' intentions. Apply consistent verification standards across political positions; do not score political actors or optimize a message to manipulate demographic groups. For evaluative passages, ask whether the author intends an ethical principle, empirical finding or prediction. If a statistical assumption is necessary, show how the conclusion depends on it and propose text that names the assumption. Criticism must be sourced and grounded, never predetermined by affiliation.

Sequence question: “At this point, which evidence establishes this conclusion, and where does a value judgment enter?” If the answer is ambiguous, propose a bridge or explicit qualification.
